Mangrove Blue Carbon Estimation (Below & Above Ground)

Mon 15 Dec 2025 • 16:19 GMT by Spatiality

Blue Carbon Mapping and Assessment. Documentation by Spatiality

Mangrove ecosystems play a crucial role in global climate mitigation by sequestering massive amounts of carbon, often referred to as blue carbon, both in their living biomass and deep sediment layers. Despite their ecological value, tracking these carbon stocks over large or remote coastal areas remains a challenge using traditional field methods alone. To overcome this, I developed a scalable geospatial workflow leveraging cloud-based remote sensing to map and estimate both above-ground and below-ground carbon stocks effectively.

The methodology is built entirely within the Google Earth Engine (GEE) environment, utilizing Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery to extract key vegetation indices. I applied a supervised machine learning approach—specifically the Random Forest algorithm—to accurately isolate and map the extent of the mangrove area. Once the mangrove mask was established, the workflow integrated global datasets for above-ground and below-ground carbon, as well as soil organic carbon, to quantify total carbon storage per hectare.

Methodology

Methodology Workflow. Documentation by Spatiality

The resulting spatial models provide a clear visualization of carbon density distribution across the landscape, with values ranging from 0 up to 150 Mg/Ha. By aggregating these values across the entire mapped zone, the GEE script computed a total estimated blue carbon stock of approximately 55,344.68 tonnes. These outputs highlight critical areas of dense carbon accumulation, offering robust, data-driven insights for conservation planning, blue carbon crediting initiatives, and monitoring the ecological health of restored or natural coastal ecosystems.
