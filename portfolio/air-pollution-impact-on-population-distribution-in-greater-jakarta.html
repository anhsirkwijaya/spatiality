Air Pollution Impact on Population Distribution in Greater Jakarta

Mon 15 Dec 2025 • 16:19 GMT by Spatiality

Air Pollution Distribution. Documentation by Spatiality

Rapid urbanization and high economic activity in metropolitan areas like Greater Jakarta often come with environmental trade-offs, most notably declining air quality. While air pollution monitoring is commonly conducted through stationary ground stations, assessing its actual human footprint requires a broader spatial perspective. To understand how population density intersects with atmospheric pollution levels, I developed a spatial analysis workflow combining satellite remote sensing and high-resolution demographic data.

Workflow

Air Pollution Impact to Population Distribution Workflow. Documentation by Spatiality

The analysis integrates multi-source datasets processed through a cloud and desktop GIS workflow:
  • Air Quality Monitoring: Extracted tropospheric gas concentrations from Sentinel-5 TROPOMI data covering key parameters, including Carbon Monoxide (CO), Formaldehyde (HCHO), Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂), Ozone (O₃), Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂), and Methane (CH₄).
  • Demographic Distribution: Utilized population grids from the Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL) to map human presence at a fine spatial resolution.
  • Regulatory Framework: Incorporated standards and thresholds from the Ministry of Environment and Forestry Regulation (Permen LHK No. P.14/MENLHK/SETJEN/KUM.1/7/2020) as a baseline reference.

The workflow begins by extracting and processing population and atmospheric data within Google Earth Engine. The processed rasters are then brought into ArcGIS Pro, where a bivariate classification technique is applied to map the spatial intersection and trade-offs between air pollution intensity and population density.

Visualization

The resulting bivariate map provides a clear depiction of vulnerable zones across Jakarta and its surrounding urban centers. By cross-referencing pollution concentrations with population grids, the map highlights areas where high-density settlements overlap with elevated pollutant levels—offering critical insights for urban planners, policymakers, and public health initiatives to formulate targeted mitigation strategies in high-risk zones.
